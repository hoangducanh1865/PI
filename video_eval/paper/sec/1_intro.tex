\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

A video world model for robot manipulation predicts the video $\hat V$ a robot would record from its first frame $I_0$ and commanded actions $a_{1:T}$; if accurate, it can replace a physics simulator for policy evaluation, planning, and data generation~\cite{yang2023unisim,zhu2024irasim,nvidia2025cosmos}.
Such models are compared mostly with frame-level appearance metrics against the recorded video, PSNR, SSIM~\cite{wang2004ssim}, and LPIPS~\cite{zhang2018lpips}, or with FVD~\cite{unterthiner2018fvd} over sets of videos.
These metrics need the real video, which a simulator does not have for new actions, and they score how frames look, not whether the robot performs the commanded motion or whether a real arm could.

Regenerating 105 Bridge~\cite{walke2023bridgedata} clips with IRASim~\cite{zhu2024irasim} from deliberately corrupted actions confirms the gap: SSIM rates a still video as high as the correct generation and above every corrupted one, whereas an end-effector track compared with the real video degrades steadily.
What a simulator always has is the commanded actions, so we check the video against them.
Inverse dynamics models have been used to read actions from generated videos and execute them, scoring task success~\cite{qin2024worldsimbench,fan2026wowwoval,zhang2026worldinworld}; such scores need a matched simulator or robot and mix the errors of the reader with those of the world model~\cite{liu2026kinebench}.
We instead compare the trajectory read from the video directly with the commanded one and measure the reader's own error on real videos.

Our main contributions are: (1) an evaluation framework that lists what each metric group measures and cannot measure, with a perturbation test of whether a metric notices wrong actions, and (2) \ours{}, a reference-free metric of action fidelity with an executability check, validated on real and corrupted videos and applied to IRASim, its baselines, and Cosmos-Predict2.5.
