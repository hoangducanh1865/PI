\section{Strengths and limitations of IRASim}
\label{sec:analysis}


\paragraph{Strengths.}
\textit{S1, the robot follows the actions.}
The gripper of Frame-Ada follows the real image path, with NDTW 0.709 and a mean error of 9.9\,px over 96 clips, and the 3-D path read back from its videos is within 1.82\,cm of the commanded one, against 1.50\,cm for the real videos.
\textit{S2, per-frame conditioning helps.}
With the same backbone and data, modulating each frame by its own action in Frame-Ada beats one embedding of the whole sequence in Video-Ada on PSNR and 2-D tracking over 105 paired clips.
\textit{S3, stable and reproducible.}
Temporal consistency is close to the real videos, two seeds give statistically equal scores, and a rerun with the same batches reproduces the videos exactly.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
\textit{L1, objects do not keep their identity.}
The most frequent violation found by the judges is an object that changes shape or vanishes, typically once the gripper holds it (\cref{fig:strips}b).
\textit{L2, correct motion does not imply the correct outcome.}
Only 50\% and 75\% of Frame-Ada clips end in the same state as the real clip according to the two judges.
\textit{L3, errors grow with contact and time.}
The gap between the cycle error of Frame-Ada and that of the real video is largest for wipe (\cref{fig:strips}c), turn, slide, and topple, at 0.5 to 0.7\,cm, and close to zero for open, flip, and fold; within a clip the error grows towards the last frames.
\textit{L4, short clips of one embodiment.}
Each checkpoint covers one dataset at $256\times320$ and 16 frames, about 3\,s; longer horizons need autoregressive chaining, which we do not test.
