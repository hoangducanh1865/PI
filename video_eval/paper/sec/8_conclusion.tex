\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On real robot data, the frame metrics used to compare video world models cannot tell whether the robot followed its actions: they rank a frozen video above generations from corrupted actions.
Trajectory scores can, and an off-the-shelf inverse dynamics model, decoded exactly as its authors encode actions, gives such a score in centimetres without any real video.
IRASim-Frame-Ada follows Bridge actions to within about 0.3\,cm of what the same reader achieves on real videos, but objects lose their identity and outcomes often differ.
Removing the action input from the same Cosmos backbone leaves smooth, plausible-looking videos whose robot moves no more correctly than a frozen frame.
Using the score to pick the best of six samples did not improve independent scores for IRASim, whose samples differ little.
