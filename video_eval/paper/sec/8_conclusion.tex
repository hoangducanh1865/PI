\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Frame-level appearance metrics cannot tell whether a generated robot video follows its actions; \ours{} can, without a real video, by reading the actions back with an off-the-shelf inverse dynamics model decoded with its authors' formulas.
On Bridge it shows that the action input matters more than the quality of the frames, that the arm motion of current models is almost always executable, and that objects are their most frequent visible failure.
Its limits are one embodiment, 105 clips harder than the average test clip, judges that disagree, and a reader whose own error of 1.50\,cm bounds what \ours{} resolves, which may have seen Bridge test episodes and may misread blurred generated frames; replaying the read motion in a simulator such as SIMPLER~\cite{li2024simpler} would add dynamics and contact.
