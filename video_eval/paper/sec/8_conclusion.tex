\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

On real robot data, frame metrics cannot tell whether the robot in a generated video followed its actions, since they rank a still video above generations from corrupted actions, while trajectory scores can.
IDM-Score gives such a score in centimetres without any real video, by decoding an off-the-shelf inverse dynamics model exactly as its authors encode actions: IRASim-Frame-Ada follows Bridge actions to within about 0.3\,cm of what the same model reads from real videos, but objects lose their identity and outcomes often differ, and the Cosmos backbone without actions moves the robot no more correctly than a still frame.
Selecting the best of six IRASim samples by IDM-Score did not improve independent scores, since the samples differ little.
The study is limited to a few hundred clips, one embodiment for IDM-Score, judges that are not stable between runs, and an inverse dynamics model whose own error of 1.50\,cm bounds what IDM-Score can resolve.
