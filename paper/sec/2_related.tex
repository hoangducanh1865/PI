\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Language-driven grasp detection.}
Grasp-Anything~\cite{vuong2023grasp} and its language extension Grasp-Anything++~\cite{vuong2024language} provide about one million synthetic scenes with grasp rectangles, part masks and prompts.
LGD~\cite{vuong2024language} and LLGD~\cite{nguyen2024lightweight} generate grasps with diffusion and consistency models; GraspMamba~\cite{nguyen2024graspmamba} fuses text and image features at several levels.
Closest to us, MaskGrasp~\cite{vo2024maskgrasp} feeds segmentation features into attention at inference and adds a triplet loss between grasp-region and mask features.
Our loss differs in three ways: it contrasts image regions with the \emph{prompt}, its negatives are the \emph{other objects of the same scene}, and masks are needed only during training.

\paragraph{Contrastive vision-language learning.}
CLIP~\cite{radford2021clip} aligns whole images and texts with the InfoNCE loss~\cite{oord2018cpc}; ALBEF~\cite{li2021albef} aligns them before fusing them with cross-attention.
We apply the same principle at the level of object regions inside one scene, where the negatives are the hardest ones: objects the robot could grasp by mistake.
