EVID: Reference-free Evaluation of Action Fidelity in Videos from Robot World Models via Inverse Dynamics

Duc Anh Hoang

PaperCode

Action-conditioned video world models predict the video a robot would record when it executes given actions, and they are evaluated mostly by how closely their frames match the recorded video. Such appearance metrics need the real video, which does not exist for new actions, and do not check whether the robot performs the commanded motion. Reproducing IRASim and its baselines and adding Cosmos-Predict2.5 on 105 Bridge and 57 Language-Table clips, we find that SSIM rates a still video as high as a correct generation and above every generation from corrupted actions. We propose EVID, which closes the loop from actions to video and back: an off-the-shelf inverse dynamics model reads the end-effector trajectory from the generated video, EVID measures its distance to the commanded trajectory, and inverse kinematics checks that the real arm could execute it. Without any real video, EVID separates real videos from a still one by a factor of six, grows monotonically with the corruption, and ranks the models as the reference-based metrics do. It shows that the action input, not the quality of the frames, decides whether the robot follows the commands, while the most frequent remaining errors are objects that change or vanish.
