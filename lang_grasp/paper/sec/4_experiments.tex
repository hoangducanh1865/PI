\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup.}
We use the official split files of the Grasp-Anything code: objects of \texttt{seen.obj} are cut 90\%/10\% into training and Seen test as in the authors' script, and \texttt{unseen.obj} is the Unseen test; one prompt is one sample (27.0k training, 3.0k Seen and 13.5k Unseen test prompts).
A prediction succeeds if its IoU with a ground-truth grasp exceeds 25\% and its angle differs by less than $30^{\circ}$~\cite{kumra2020antipodal}; H is the harmonic mean of Seen and Unseen.
Training follows the authors' code (Adam, learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch 8, 50 epochs of 1,000 batches) on two T4 GPUs with data parallelism for both models; $\lambda = 0.1$ and $\tau = 0.07$ are not tuned.
For 70\% (Seen) and 83\% (Unseen) of 1,000 random multi-part test objects, all part prompts have near-identical grasp centres (within 5 pixels): the labels are mostly object-level.

\paragraph{Results.}
Under identical training (\cref{tab:main}), the contrastive loss improves the baseline by 2.0 points on Seen, 7.5 points on Unseen and 5.8 points of H.
Most of the gain comes from fewer missed predictions (no grasp on 11.5\% to 4.9\% of Unseen prompts), while wrong-object grasps drop only slightly (35.5\% to 33.7\%, \cref{tab:errors}), so grounding remains the main weakness.
